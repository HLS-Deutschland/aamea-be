Africa's resources need a trusted connector, not another report

  • Geology and demand are there. What is missing is a partner every side trusts.
  • Whoever fills that gap becomes the entry point to the sector.
  • The strongest positions in African resources were built over years, not won in one deal.

How we work

  • Ready before the opening. We build capability first and announce later.
  • The state owns the outcome. We supply what the state lacks.
  • Finance, logistics and market access, together. Each alone is replaceable.
  • Institutions, not individuals. We build relationships that survive a change of leadership.

What AAMEA brings

For governments

Trust of industry and of partner governments when a project crosses borders. A credible counterpart on both sides of the table.

For industry

Trust in a reliable government counterpart and predictable permitting, and that opportunities are spotted and communicated early.

For financiers

Trust in the projects we recommend, and the ability to close gaps on the way to readiness.

For partners

Trust in a curated circle of international companies and governments, with access to deals and the option to co-invest as the platform matures.

What this makes possible

Governments sell and process more of their own resources.

Projects reach financing on clearer terms.

Capital finds one trusted entry point instead of many unreliable ones.

Partners who join early help shape how it works.

Annex · Examples from the field

Examples: how positions in African resources were built

Public cases from African resources. They are not AAMEA projects and not a forecast of AAMEA's results; they show the patterns AAMEA builds on. Figures are from public reporting; sources at the end.

A · Gécamines and Mercuria: a state starts selling its own metal (Congo)

2024: Congo sells part of its share of joint-venture copper for the first time. The September 2024 Tenke tender is reported to go half to Mercuria, the rest to Glencore and Trafigura. Mercuria hires Kostas Bintas, formerly co-head of metals at Trafigura, to build a metals desk. It was already marketing some Gécamines copper before the formal venture.

December 2025: Gécamines and Mercuria launch a joint venture to market copper, cobalt and other critical minerals. Gécamines controls it; Mercuria provides finance, logistics and technical support. The US–DRC strategic partnership is signed in the same period.

12 January 2026: Gécamines exercises its right to buy 100,000 t of Tenke Fungurume's 2026 copper (about 20%, equal to its stake) for sale to the United States.

February 2026: marketing rights to part of Glencore's KCC output. Leadership of Gécamines changes on 23 February.

19 October 2026: Kipushi zinc concentrate offtake of up to 240,000 t over two years.

Reading: a state moved from collecting dividends to selling metal, because a partner had spent two years building capacity first. The leadership change shows why relationships must rest on institutions.
B · Lobito Corridor: logistics that shape a region (Angola; links to Congo and Zambia)

Scope: the concession covers the Benguela railway in Angola, from the port of Lobito to the Congolese border. The line continues in Congo; Zambia's connection is financed separately (African Development Bank).

2022: Angola awards a 30-year concession to Trafigura, Mota-Engil and Vecturis, over a Chinese bidder: upfront fee, new locomotives and wagons, freight target of 1.67 million t a year by year five.

December 2025: the US Development Finance Corporation signs a loan of up to US$553 million; financial close reported for mid-2026.

Reading: whoever moves the cargo shapes the corridor; trading, logistics and finance reinforce each other, and government-backed capital follows.
C · Kamoa-Kakula: capital, technology and the state, each at the right moment (Congo)

2015: Zijin buys 49.5% of the Kamoa holding company for US$412 million, paid in instalments. 2016: partnership with the Congolese government, which holds 20%.

2018: CITIC Metal becomes Ivanhoe's largest shareholder, described by Ivanhoe as the result of a 15-year relationship. 2021: first concentrate scheduled at Kakula.

Reading: long relationships come before the contract; each partner enters at a defined moment.
D · Two further examples

Orion Critical Mineral Consortium (US government finance and Abu Dhabi's ADQ; US$1.8 billion, October 2025): in February 2026 a non-binding memorandum on a possible 40% stake in Glencore's Mutanda and KCC interests.

Africa Finance Corporation (founded 2007 by African states, rated A): a mandate from member states and neutrality are assets that accumulate slowly.

E · Seven ingredients of a connector position, drawn from these examples
  1. Capability before the opening
  2. A state counterparty that owns the outcome
  3. A political window, read early
  4. Finance, logistics and market access combined
  5. People with a record on the other side of the table
  6. Patient capital, staged commitments
  7. Resilience to change at the counterparty

In every case above the position took years to build; the largest results came after a decade or more of relationships.

What AAMEA brings, and why

Statements on AAMEA's own offer. Each states its status plainly: design and offer, not a record of completed work.

F1 · For governments: a credible counterpart on both sides of the table
Mechanism
AAMEA is African-led and holds a neutral position between governments, industry and capital. For a ministry it combines predictive targeting of higher-probability zones, a single view of land, rights and protected areas, two to four joint policy sessions a year with investor feedback, and energy-linked project structuring. Its flagship programme places an expert team inside a ministry for 100 days to work on permitting, GIS, environmental-assessment systems and digital transparency.
Why trust
Industry meets a government that has been through a structured reform and is pre-briefed; the government meets investors who have been prepared. A partner government can state in a letter that it works with AAMEA, which signals to others that projects will move.
Status
Offer and design. Delivery is not claimed here.
F2 · For industry: predictable permitting, early sight of opportunities
Mechanism
Digital permitting and one-stop approvals are the stated aims of the 100-Day programme, with a target of faster approvals. Predictive analytics rank areas by likelihood of success, so opportunities are identified before they are tendered, and the policy sessions let industry feed into rule changes.
Status
Targets and design. Permitting outcomes depend on the government concerned.
F3 · For financiers: projects that reach you on a common scale
Mechanism
AAMEA assesses project documents on one scale: twelve dimensions on a five-level maturity ladder, six knockout criteria and four decision bands (Reject, Park, Develop, Advance). Gaps found are made visible, and AAMEA works with the sponsor to close them before a project is put forward.
Limits
AAMEA recommends on its own judgement; it is not a guarantee, and the financier decides.
F4 · For partners: a curated circle, with access that grows as the platform does
Mechanism
AAMEA builds a curated circle of international companies and governments around its work. Access to deals and co-investment grows as the platform matures.
Status
Offer. Deals and co-investment are options, not promises.
